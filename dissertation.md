**Title:** The Subregular Complexity of Case and Agreement

**Abstract**

This dissertation aims to establish a tight bound on the computational complexity of *case* and *agreement* dependencies in syntax, that is, the class of possible formal patterns which they exemplify. Such dependencies are shown to be modeled well by a tree-based generalization of of *tier-based strictly 2-local* (TSL-2) formal languages, mirroring past results on long-distance phonotactics and syntactic movement.

This characterization has several important consequences. First, TSL-2 is a highly restrictive formal class, ruling out many unnatural and presumably impossible patterns whose absence is left unexplained by earlier, string-based classifications of syntactic patterns. Second, the range of patterns it can express aligns closely with well-known cross-linguistic generalizations and points of variation, unifying many phenomena previously handled by distinct theoretical mechanisms. Third, TSL-2 patterns can be computed by simple, cognitively plausible pattern-detection mechanisms, explaining why it is that language makes use of these and not others. Finally, the class of TSL-2 patterns is in principle efficiently learnable, providing a crucial hint into how human children learn languages so effortlessly, and by extension, how we might design artificial systems that do the same.

All necessary background on subregular formal languages and their application to syntax is included, though some familiarity with Minimalist syntax is assumed. As such, this dissertation may also serve as an introduction to subregular complexity for both theoretical and computational linguists alike.
